<a class="feat-hero" href="https://jachacks.org/">JacHacks SF: 200 builders, 9 hours, 77 AI apps built in Jac</a>

<a href="https://jachacks.org/" target="_blank" rel="noopener" class="news-img">
  <img src="/issues/005/jachacks-sf-newsletter-flyer.png" alt="JacHacks SF recap: San Francisco's first JacHacks is officially in the books. 200+ builders, 9 hours of building, 77 AI apps shipped in Jac, across Agentic AI, Fintech, and Social Impact." />
</a>

<p class="feat-lede">JacHacks came to San Francisco for the first time on <strong>July 26</strong>, taking over Founders, Inc. for a single day of building. More than <strong>200 builders</strong> shipped <strong>77 finished AI projects</strong> in Jac in <strong>under 9 hours</strong>, competing across three tracks: Agentic AI, Fintech, and Social Impact.</p>

<p class="feat-lede">The 77 projects covered a wide range of use cases, across industries from healthcare to fintech to disaster response, and every one of them was built in Jac in that same 9 hours, a genuinely tall order for a single sitting.</p>

<p class="feat-lede">Jac is what made the timeline realistic: one language covers the backend, the database, and the frontend, and <code>by llm()</code> adds AI to any function with no prompt engineering required. Builders also had the opportunity to host and deploy their apps through <a href="https://jachammer.ai/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Jac Hammer</a>.</p>

## Articles

### [Hijac: how the JacHacks Winter Social Impact winners put autonomous Jac agents on a phone](https://blogs.jaseci.org/blog/posts/hijac-how-the-jachacks-winter-social-good-winners-put-autonomous-jac-agents-on-a-phone)

Nitin Shankar Madhu, Chuka Ezeoke, and Meron took 1st in the Social Good track at JacHacks Winter with Hijac, a pipeline that compiles Jac apps straight into installable native mobile apps. Their demo: an agent that watches a phone's sensors, judges whether a spike looks like a fall, and calls an emergency contact on its own if the owner doesn't respond within 30 seconds. It runs entirely on Object-Spatial Programming, with sensor readings modeled as nodes and `by llm()` handling the judgment call with no JSON-parsing boilerplate.

## Releases

### [jaclang 0.34: native by default](https://docs.jaseci.org/community/release_notes/jaclang/)

What matters if you're upgrading:

- **Native is now the default codespace.** The old opt-in `--autonative` system is gone, and the compiler infers the client codespace from the structure of your code.
- **Markerless routing.** Route co-location in `pages/` no longer needs markers, and `JsxPage` / `JsxLayout` types decide route membership instead of filename conventions.
- **KEDA autoscaling.** HTTP activation resources ship for scale-to-zero deployments, and `jac start --scale` can pin a deployment to a specific version.
- **React Native npm dependencies** are managed like any other dep, and native code can now be imported into client code as WebAssembly.
